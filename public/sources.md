# Sources and Visual Credits

These are companion references, not additional slides. All simulations are local and fictional.

## 1. NIST: Blockchain Technology Overview (2018)

https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8202/final

NISTIR 8202. Distributed ledgers, blocks, hashes, consensus, permission models, security, and limitations. The final report, not the earlier draft, is used.

## 2. Satoshi Nakamoto: Bitcoin paper (2008)

https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf

Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System. Primary source for double spending, hash-linked records, and proof of work; not a specification for every blockchain.

## 3. Haber and Stornetta: How to Time-Stamp a Digital Document (1991)

https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00196791

Stuart Haber and W. Scott Stornetta, Journal of Cryptology 3, 99-111. Early linked timestamping research, not a claim that Bitcoin existed in 1991.

## 4. Encyclopaedia Britannica: Satoshi Nakamoto

https://www.britannica.com/biography/Satoshi-Nakamoto

The name used by the author or authors of the Bitcoin paper; their identity is unknown. No speculative identity is presented.

## 5. Laszlo Hanyecz: Pizza for bitcoins? (2010)

https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=137.0

Original May 2010 forum thread. Hanyecz confirmed exchanging 10,000 bitcoin for pizza on May 22; his offer was for two pizzas. Another participant arranged the pizzas. No current valuation is used.

## 6. Ethereum Foundation: Ethereum Launches (July 30, 2015)

https://blog.ethereum.org/2015/07/30/ethereum-launches

Primary announcement of Frontier, the first live Ethereum release.

## 7. Ethereum documentation: Proof of work

https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pow/

Computational work, block proposals, verification, and energy use. Ethereum no longer uses proof of work; the slide compares mechanisms, not current Ethereum and Bitcoin energy statistics.

## 8. Ethereum documentation: Proof of stake

https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/

Validators, staked assets, proposals, attestations, and network-specific penalties. Not all mistakes cause slashing, and not all proof-of-stake networks use identical rules.

## 9. Ethereum documentation: Introduction to smart contracts

https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/smart-contracts/

Programs on a blockchain that execute when called. Programmed conditions do not imply legal fairness, correctness, or an independent understanding of the real world.

## 10. Ethereum documentation: Oracles

https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/oracles/

External data bridges and the oracle problem. The flight example is entirely fictional; no airline, insurance product, API, or live feed is used.

## 11. Walmart: 2018 Global Responsibility Report Summary

https://stock.walmart.com/_assets/_8521831b8d70c598f8c9e787a008f80a/walmart/db/951/9698/file/WMT-2018-GRR-Summary-for-digital.pdf

Walmart reported six days, 18 hours, and 26 minutes to trace a package of sliced mangoes with existing methods, versus 2.2 seconds in its blockchain pilot. These are reported record-retrieval times, not an independent trial, a universal speed claim, or proof of input accuracy. The clickable supply-chain records in this deck are invented teaching examples, not pilot data.

## 12. MIT Registrar: Digital diplomas

https://registrar.mit.edu/transcripts-records/diplomas/digital-diplomas

MIT offers opt-in Blockcerts digital diplomas and a verification portal. Recipients should retain their credential file and access information. MIT describes reissuance if access is lost; this is different from losing self-managed wallet keys.

## 13. Blockcerts: Verification process

https://www.blockcerts.org/guide/verification-process.html

Credential integrity, blockchain anchoring, issuer identity, and revocation checks. The local Ed25519 classroom demo illustrates signatures; it does not reproduce the complete Blockcerts verification protocol.

## 14. Andy Greenberg, WIRED: The IronKey story (October 24, 2023)

https://www.wired.com/story/unciphered-ironkey-password-cracking-bitcoin/

Reports Stefan Thomas's account of an encrypted IronKey holding keys to 7,002 bitcoin and eight unsuccessful attempts out of ten. The story also discusses recovery research. This deck does not claim that recovery is impossible or establish his present-day access status. No dollar valuation is shown.

## 15. Crypto Carbon Ratings Institute: Ethereum Energy Study (2022)

https://carbon-ratings.com/eth-report-2022

Documented an estimated 99.988% to 99.95% electrical power and carbon reduction when Ethereum migrated from Proof of Work to Proof of Stake during The Merge in September 2022.

## 16. Visa & Blockchain Throughput Benchmarks

https://usa.visa.com/solutions/crypto/deep-dive-on-solana.html

Visa processing capability exceeds 24,000 transactions per second (handling ~2,000 avg TPS), compared to Bitcoin L1 (5-7 TPS) and Ethereum L1 (15-30 TPS). Centralized databases exceed 10,000-100,000 TPS.
